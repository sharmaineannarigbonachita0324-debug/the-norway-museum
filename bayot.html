A digital museum of Sámi heritage in Norway

Bures boahtin.Welcome, in Northern Sámi.

Long before Norway had a border, the Sámi lived across the far north, following reindeer, fish and seasons through a land they call Sápmi. This museum gathers their objects, words and history in one place, open to anyone, anywhere.

The museum

History is held in hands.

It lives in a cup carved from a birch burl, in the tin thread on a collar, in a drum that crossed the sea to Copenhagen and came home three hundred years later.

The Sámi are the Indigenous people of the northern Nordic countries and Russia's Kola Peninsula, and the largest share of them live in Norway. Here you will find their past and present told through objects and the knowledge behind them. Choose any object to open its gallery label.

Three rooms

Rooms of memory.

Each room follows one thread of Sámi history, from the oldest marks on the rock to the culture being made today.

Room I · Deep time

Signs on the rock

Reindeer, elk, boats and people carved into the stone at Alta, and the first written record of Sámi life, told to an English king around 890.

Room II · Belief and loss

The drum and the silence

Noaidi drums, the missionary campaigns that seized them, and a century of school policy that tried to silence the Sámi languages.

Room III · Today

Living Sápmi

Duodji, joik, language revival and a parliament of their own. A culture that is worn, sung and argued over every day.

The living museum

A culture still in motion.

Sámi heritage does not stay behind glass. It is joiked at weddings, stitched for confirmation day, taught in Sámi-language kindergartens and debated in parliament. Objects tell part of the story. People tell the rest.

Joik

One of Europe's oldest living song traditions. A joik does not describe a person or a place; it calls them into the room.

Language

Northern Sámi is the most widely spoken Sámi language. Lule and South Sámi are far smaller, and teachers, apps and broadcasters are working to keep them in daily use.

Reindeer

Herding is still central to Sámi identity, though only a small share of Sámi today make their living from it. In most of Norway the right to herd belongs to Sámi alone.

Sea Sámi

Along the fjords of Finnmark and Troms, coastal Sámi fished and farmed for centuries. Their history is part of the story too.

Through time

Centuries, in context.

Eight moments in Sámi history, from carved stone to a returned drum.

  1. c. 4200–500 BCE

    Carvings at Alta

    Hunter and fishing communities of the far north carve thousands of figures into the rock at Alta, Finnmark. The site joins the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1985.

  2. c. 890

    Ottar's account

    The northern chieftain Ottar visits King Alfred in England and describes the Sámi, whom he calls Finnas, paying tribute in furs, feathers and whalebone. It is one of the earliest written records of Sámi life.

  3. 1600s–1700s

    Drums are taken

    Lutheran missionary campaigns target Sámi belief. Noaidi drums are confiscated and many are burned. In 1723 one drum is seized in Bindal and sent to Copenhagen.

  4. 1751

    The Lapp Codicil

    An addition to the border treaty between Denmark-Norway and Sweden protects the right of reindeer herders to cross the new border with their herds.

  5. c. 1850–1960s

    Norwegianization

    State policy pushes Sámi children into Norwegian-only schooling and boarding schools. Generations grow up without their own language.

  6. 6 Feb 1917

    The first Sámi congress

    Sámi from Norway and Sweden meet in Trondheim, organised in part by Elsa Laula Renberg. The date becomes Sámi National Day.

  7. 1979–1989

    From protest to parliament

    Plans to dam the Alta River spark protests, a lávvu outside the Storting and hunger strikes. The struggle leads to the Sámi Act of 1987 and the opening of the Sámi Parliament, Sámediggi, in 1989.

  8. 2023

    A drum comes home

    The drum seized in 1723 returns from Denmark to Saemien Sijte in Snåsa. The same year, a Truth and Reconciliation Commission delivers its report on Norwegianization to the Storting.

Words to carry

Eight Sámi words.

Northern Sámi terms you will meet across this museum, with what they mean.

Sápminoun · place

The Sámi homeland, stretching across Norway, Sweden, Finland and Russia.

duodjinoun · craft

Sámi handicraft: practical objects made with skill, from knives to clothing.

gáktinoun · dress

Traditional clothing whose colours and cut show where the wearer comes from.

luohtinoun · song

A joik. To joik is juoigat.

siidanoun · community

A group of families who herd and move together across shared land.

lávvunoun · shelter

A portable tent of poles and cloth, raised in minutes.

noaidinoun · person

A spiritual specialist who could travel between worlds, often with a drum.

guksinoun · object

A drinking cup carved from a single birch burl.
